How it protects you

End-to-end

Keys never hit our servers

Each broadcast's decryption key is made in your browser and lives only in the part of the share link after the #. By web standard, browsers never send that part to a server — so it stays out of the relays, proxies, and access logs that carry your video. It's only as private as how you share the link, though: whoever carries your message — and your own browser history — sees the whole link, so hand it out with care.

No directory

Discovery on a DHT

Your stream is found by its public key via the BitTorrent/pkarr DHT. There's no directory to browse — a stream is reachable only to someone who already has its key.

Open & pinned

Code you can read

The two pages load one open-source client, pinned by a hash so it can't be swapped under you. Read exactly what runs, host your own copy on any web server, or open send.html from your own computer.

Relay-blind

The network can't decode

The relays that carry your video, and the broker that assigns them, never see the content key. They shuttle encrypted frames and nothing more.

How a broadcast flows

  1. Open send.html, allow your camera — your browser mints a keypair and a content key.
  2. It asks the broker for a relay and publishes its public key → relay to the DHT.
  3. You share a link to view.html that carries the pubkey and the #… key.
  4. A viewer's browser resolves the pubkey on the DHT, gets a nearby relay, and decrypts locally.

The broker (tinymoq.com) assigns relays and mints the short-lived token that lets your browser use them — it can't decrypt anything. Read the full trust model →

What each part can see

You

Your browser

Sees everything — the live picture and the keys. The video is encrypted here before it leaves, and decrypted here when it arrives. Nothing in between can do either.

Fleet

The relays

Pass your video along as encrypted data they can't unlock. While you're connected they see your IP address (any server you talk to does) — but it's never written to a log or a database.

Control

The broker

Picks a nearby relay and issues a short-lived pass to use it. It never gets your decryption key and never sees your video. It sees your IP on the request but doesn't store it — to keep bots from hammering the API it counts requests against a one-way hash of your IP (never the address itself), and that clears in about two minutes. It keeps no record of which streams ran.

Discovery

The public directory (DHT)

Holds one thing: your public key → which relay you're on, so viewers can find your stream. A directory node sees the IP that publishes or looks up a key. No video, no viewer list.

The key

Your share link

The bit after the # in your link is the decryption key. Browsers never send that part anywhere, so only the people you give the link to can watch.

The app

The client code

Open source and hash-pinned — no analytics, no trackers, no identity mining. Pinned means it can't quietly change; open means you can read exactly what it does. The honest limit: proving the pinned build matches its source needs a reproducible build — that's on the roadmap.

The one thing every hop can see is your IP address — that's true of visiting any website, not something earthseed adds. We never store it, but a relay can't help seeing the address it's exchanging packets with. If you'd rather it didn't, put a VPN or Tor in front — encryption and discovery work exactly the same. Which relay carries your video

Default · works today

The shared fleet

Your owned copy carries a public pk_… key and asks the broker (tinymoq.com) for a nearby relay — zero setup, relays provided. You own the app; the relay network is still ours. The broker never sees your key or your video.

Roadmap · bring your own

Point at any MoQ relay

A config switch (coming) will let the same bundle talk straight to an open relay — no broker, no key. Use Cloudflare Media over QUIC, moq.pro (free up to 500 GB), or run moq.dev's moq-relay yourself. Then no part of the path is ours.

Honest status: today an owned copy still calls the broker to get a relay and to find streams on the DHT — the code is yours, the relay is still provided. Full independence (your own relay, no broker) is the next step, and more relay options are coming to the broker too. See the trust model →
